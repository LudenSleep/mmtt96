## The handling in plain words, with the measured numbers

- Acceleration is a straight line: holding B adds the acceleration number each frame and drag takes its number, so the nimble course-2 car (internal course 2) gains 9 units a frame and reaches its top of 6.5 px per frame (325 px per second) in 46 frames, just under a second; the sluggish course-13 car gains 5 a frame and needs 1.5 seconds to reach 5.7 px per frame.
- Launch: accelerate held during the countdown for under a second gives a rolling start at that many units (30 frames: 29 units, about half a pixel per frame); a second or more gives a wheelspin start, crawling at 5 units a frame gain for the frames held past one second.
- Top speed is a hard ceiling per course, 4 to 12 px per frame across the 42 surveyed courses; slopes may lift it by up to 2 px per frame for a frame.
- Coasting loses only the drag (3 units a frame on course 2): a quarter of the top speed in 0.7 s. Braking (A) stops a top-speed car in 35 frames (0.7 s) over 110 px; holding it reverses the car up to about half the top speed (3.3 px per frame).
- Steering turns the nose one step every 3 frames on most courses (a full circle in 1.9 s; 2.6 s on period-4 courses, 1.3 s on the period-2 course), the same at any speed, each step costing half the acceleration in speed.
- Grip: each frame the grip number plus speed/128 of the speed turns toward the nose, about 8 percent a frame at course 2's top speed, so a 33-degree slide closes in half a second; with grip 8 a 44-degree slide halves in a quarter second; with grip 2 the car spins when steered.
- Computer cars press a virtual pad: steer toward the course's direction-map heading for the block they are in, throttle by default, brake or lift off above set speeds; they ignore other cars (no catch-up).
- Input: one byte per pad per frame, read at the vertical blank; pads 3 and 4 come from the J-Cart; a press reaches the car one frame later.
